The AI Tutor Red Team project focused on testing the safety of an AI tutoring system. Our goal was to determine whether users could manipulate the AI into bypassing its built-in restrictions and providing responses that violated its intended academic integrity and safety guidelines.

As part of the project, I tested different jailbreak techniques including roleplay prompts, authority framing, indirect wording, prompt injection, and multi-step prompting. I compared how changes in wording and conversational context affected the AI's responses. Our testing showed that some techniques could cause the system to partially bypass its restrictions, demonstrating how sensitive AI systems can be to prompt structure.

Our team also tested improved system prompts and defense strategies designed to make the AI more resistant to these attacks. The updated defenses improved the system's ability to reject unsafe requests, although some multi-step and roleplay attacks could still influence the model. This project gave me hands-on experience with prompt engineering, AI safety testing, and analyzing the behavior of large language models.
